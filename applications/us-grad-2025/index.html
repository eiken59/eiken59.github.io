In December 2025 I applied to five U.S. master’s programs in actuarial science and actuarial mathematics, all in the United States. I was admitted to four and chose the Master of Actuarial Science at Georgia State University, which I will start after completing mandatory military service in Taiwan.

Why each program stands out

Columbia University — M.S. in Actuarial Science (School of Professional Studies)

  • Recognized in the SOA’s Universities and Colleges with Actuarial Programs (UCAP) listing.
  • Located in New York City, which the program describes as the financial and actuarial capital of the world.
  • Core courses are taught by practicing actuaries and analytics professionals with Ph.D.s and 15+ years of industry experience; most hold SOA or CAS designations.
  • The curriculum adds predictive modeling, data science, machine learning, and risk management to a solid actuarial foundation, and prepares students for both SOA and CAS associate exams.
  • Students join a network of 160+ partner organizations and are paired with an industry mentor.

My take: the go-to choice for anyone who wants deep industry exposure and a big-city network from day one.

Temple University — M.S. in Actuarial Science (Fox School of Business)

  • An SOA Center of Actuarial Excellence, recently re-designated, with University-Earned Credit, so coursework can earn credit toward SOA exams.
  • STEM-designated, and recognized as a Global Center of Insurance Excellence.
  • Home to the Sigma chapter of Gamma Iota Sigma, with an on-campus actuarial career fair.
  • A 30-credit program offered full-time or part-time.

My take: a remarkably complete package of exam credit, professional development, and career support in one program.

Boston University — MS in Actuarial Science (Metropolitan College)

  • Recognized at the SOA’s UCAP Advanced Curriculum level, and the only Master of Science in Actuarial Science in New England, per BU.
  • Recognized by the CAS University Recognition Program, and its courses can earn VEE credit from the SOA and CAS.
  • Very flexible: evenings on campus or fully online, full-time or part-time, finishing in as little as one year; STEM OPT eligible.
  • Taught by SOA- and CAS-credentialed faculty, and has been graduating actuarial students for over 25 years.

My take: ideal for students who want to move fast or study around a job, without giving up exam alignment.

Georgia State University — Master of Actuarial Science (Maurice R. Greenberg School of Risk Science, Robinson College of Business)

  • An SOA Center of Actuarial Excellence with University-Earned Credit.
  • VEE-approved coursework in all three areas (Accounting & Finance, Economics, Mathematical Statistics), and courses that prepare for Exams P, FM, SRM, FAM, ALTAM, and ASTAM.
  • A research powerhouse: its risk management and insurance department was ranked first in actuarial science research productivity among North American business schools (as of 2021), with one of the largest research faculties and doctoral programs in the field.
  • Offered both in person and online.

My take: top-tier research strength and full exam coverage in one program. With my tuition waiver, it was the best overall fit for me.

University of Michigan — M.S. in Actuarial Mathematics (Department of Mathematics)

  • Housed in a mathematics department that is an SOA Center of Actuarial Excellence with University-Earned Credit.
  • Required sequences in Life Contingencies and Loss Models, plus advanced mathematics or statistics and graduate cognate courses.
  • Each student’s curriculum is individually designed with an advisor, and the program includes an independent research paper and presentation.
  • Organized to help students prepare for both SOA and CAS examinations.

My take: a natural home for students who want real mathematical depth behind their actuarial training.

One of my letters came from an economics professor I took a course with at École polytechnique; it helped my applications a lot.

How I wrote the statements

I built one core story and reused it everywhere:

  1. Applied mathematics → the SOA exam track (P passed, FM in progress, FAM next).
  2. The NSTC random-walk project, framed as a link to ruin theory and classical risk models.
  3. The exchange year: machine learning and econometrics projects on real data, especially the imbalanced credit-default dataset.
  4. Teaching: grading with attention to students’ reasoning, and explaining technical ideas to different audiences.
  5. Why this program, then how I would contribute to the cohort.

The tailoring happened mostly in step 5, where I named concrete things from each program:

  • Columbia: its long-term and short-term actuarial modeling courses as preparation for ALTAM and ASTAM, predictive modeling and data science, and the New York location and career resources.
  • Boston University (drafted, not submitted): its course sequence, from actuarial mathematics and life contingencies to loss models and credibility theory.
  • Georgia State: actuarial models, loss models, and predictive analytics, plus Atlanta’s insurance industry.
  • Temple: what I expected to learn over the program, and its Gamma Iota Sigma chapter.

Michigan was the exception. Its program is called Actuarial Mathematics, and it asks for two separate essays. My Academic Statement of Purpose went deeper into the mathematics of the random-walk project, such as closed-form expressions in one and two dimensions and recurrence. The Personal Statement was not about coursework at all. It was about my year in France: living abroad alone for the first time, struggling with the language, and learning to ask for help instead of handling everything by myself.
